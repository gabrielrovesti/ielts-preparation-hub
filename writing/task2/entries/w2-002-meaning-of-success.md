# The Meaning of Success

## Prompt

Many people try to achieve success through their career or education.

What can success mean to different people? What is your view of success?

Write at least 250 words.

## Model answer (Band 8-9, ~270 words)

Success is a notoriously elastic concept, and its meaning shifts considerably depending on an individual's values, cultural background, and stage of life.

For many people, success is measured in conventional, externally verifiable terms: career advancement, income, academic credentials, or social status. This view dominates in societies where achievement is closely tied to visible markers — a job title, a salary, a degree from a prestigious institution — because these markers are easy to compare and, rightly or wrongly, function as shorthand for a life well-lived. For others, particularly those with strong family or community ties, success is measured relationally: raising children well, maintaining close friendships, or being someone others can depend on in difficult times. Neither definition is more "correct" than the other; they simply reflect different value systems.

My own view sits closer to the second definition, though not entirely. I would define success as the ongoing alignment between what someone values and how they actually spend their time and energy — a definition that can, in principle, accommodate both the executive who genuinely loves their work and the teacher who measures their year by the students they have helped rather than by their modest salary. What I resist is the assumption, common in competitive academic and professional environments, that success is a fixed external scoreboard to be climbed; this framing tends to produce achievement without satisfaction; people who tick every conventional box yet feel no closer to whatever they were actually chasing.

Ultimately, I believe success is best understood as a private, ongoing negotiation rather than a public destination. A career milestone or qualification can be part of that negotiation, but treating it as the whole of the answer risks mistaking the scoreboard for the game.
