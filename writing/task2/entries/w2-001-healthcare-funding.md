# Government-Funded Healthcare

## Prompt

Some people believe that healthcare should be funded directly by the government, while others believe individuals should pay for their own medical treatment.

Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Write at least 250 words.

## Gabriel's original essay (verbatim, personal-session-result)

The question of whether healthcare should be funded directly by the state or paid for by individuals is one of the most debated issues of recent years. I recognise that this represents a significant financial burden for governments, which would be required to cover substantial costs independently, but I largely agree that access to healthcare is a fundamental right and that states bear a primary responsibility to ensure it is available to all citizens, regardless of income.

The strongest argument in favour of state-funded healthcare is one of equity. When medical treatment depends on personal wealth, those on lower incomes are effectively penalised for falling ill, often delaying care until conditions worsen — at greater cost to society overall. Italy provides a relevant example: as a country with a publicly funded health system, it offers most services free of charge to citizens, with only modest co-payments required for specialist consultations, while the majority of prescribed medications are subsidised or fully reimbursed by the state. This model demonstrates that universal coverage can reduce inequality and improve population health outcomes, provided the system is adequately funded and managed.

However, a fully free system funded by the state is not without its drawbacks. Healthcare is very expensive and not all nations may be able to provide equally this service — for example, in developing countries, it may be hard since the fiscal capacity may be limited and providing comprehensive coverage is not without its limits. This can also lead to overuse — if non direct costs are demanded to the individual, demand can quickly exceed supply, leading to longer wait times. Usually, considering once again the Italy example, a free system may be particularly demanding for the personnel, since many times long wait times are the norm and this leads many people resorting to private healthcare with much higher costs.

In conclusion, I believe that basic medical care should be provided free of charge by the state, as no person should be denied treatment on financial grounds. The ideal balance should be a pragmatica approach combining public funding with modest co-payments for non-essential services may represent the most sustainable and equitable solution in practice.
