# Festival Volunteering

## Prompt

You have heard that one of your friends will be doing voluntary work at a festival.

Write a letter to your friend. In your letter:
- ask about what type of festival your friend is volunteering at and when and where it will take place
- ask what your friend will have to do as part of the voluntary work
- ask if the organisers are still looking for more volunteers and how you can apply for the work

Write at least 150 words. You do NOT need to write any addresses. Begin: *Dear Pat,*

## Model answer (Band 8-9, ~185 words)

Dear Pat,

I only just heard from Sam that you've signed up to volunteer at a festival this summer — that's brilliant news! Is it one of the music festivals over near the coast, or something more local, like a food or arts event? And when exactly is it happening — I'm trying to work out whether I could come down and see you in action, or at least catch you afterwards for a drink.

I'm curious what they've actually got you doing. Is it front-of-house stuff — stewarding, checking tickets, that sort of thing — or are you behind the scenes helping with setup and logistics? Either way, I imagine it'll be exhausting but a good laugh, especially if you get to work alongside people who've done it for years.

One more thing: do you know if they're still short on volunteers? I've got a free week around then and wouldn't mind giving it a go myself, so if there's still space, let me know how the application actually works — is it a quick online form, or do they want a chat first?

Looking forward to hearing all about it.

Best,
Alex

## Examiner notes

- **Task Achievement**: all three bullets covered with a genuine follow-up layer each (not just the base question) — e.g. type of festival *and* a concrete guess ("music festivals over near the coast") that invites correction rather than a flat yes/no.
- **Coherence and Cohesion**: natural progression para-by-para, no mechanical "Firstly / Secondly."
- **Lexical Resource**: idiomatic informal register ("that's brilliant news," "a good laugh," "short on volunteers") without reaching for rare vocabulary.
- **Grammar**: varied clause structures (conditional, embedded questions, ellipsis in "Either way, I imagine...").
