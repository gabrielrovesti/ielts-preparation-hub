# Describe a Significant Childhood Memory

## Cue card

Describe a memory from your childhood that you still think about.

You should say:
- what happened
- where and when it happened
- who else was involved

and explain why this memory has stayed with you.

## Model answer (Band 8-8.5, ~1.5-2 min spoken)

The memory that comes to mind is the first time I got a computer to actually do something I'd told it to do — which sounds like a strange thing to be nostalgic about, but bear with me.

I must have been about ten, and my father had an old desktop machine that technically belonged to his work but ended up living in our spare room. I'd been poking around at it for weeks, mostly breaking things, until one afternoon I got a tiny program to print something back at me on screen — nothing complicated, just a line of text, but it felt like I'd made something out of nothing. My father was in and out of the room the whole time, half-amused and half-concerned I was going to break the machine entirely.

What's stayed with me isn't really the technical achievement itself — it was laughably basic — but the specific feeling of cause and effect finally clicking into place: I write this, and that happens, reliably, every time. I think that moment is probably the actual root of why I ended up studying computer science years later, even though at the time it was just messing around on a rainy afternoon rather than anything I'd have called a decision.

It also reminds me now of how patient my father was about the whole thing, given that I was, by any reasonable measure, actively risking a piece of equipment that wasn't even ours.

## Examiner notes

- Cue card fully covered but the "why it stayed with me" element is genuinely analytical (cause-effect realisation, career root) rather than a generic "it made me happy" — this is the single biggest differentiator at Band 8+ for this question type.
- Self-aware framing ("which sounds like a strange thing... but bear with me") signals natural spoken register rather than a memorised script.
- Past habitual and past continuous used correctly and distinctly ("had been poking around," "was in and out of the room").
